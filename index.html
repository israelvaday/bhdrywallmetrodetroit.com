Buyer's guide

How to hire a drywall contractor in Metro Detroit

A good drywall bid is specific. A bad one is a single number with no finish level, no scope, and no start date. Use this checklist before you sign.

1. Verify license and insurance. Ask for proof of general liability and workers comp. For commercial jobs, request a certificate naming your building or GC as additional insured.

2. Define finish level. Level 4 vs Level 5 changes labor cost more than board cost. Put it in the quote.

3. Texture match in writing. "Match existing" should name the style (orange peel, knockdown, smooth) and include a sample board when possible.

4. Separate hang, finish, and paint. Know who primes. Many paint failures are skipped primer on new mud — clarify before close-in.

5. Schedule and access. Occupied homes need daily cleanup and dust control. Commercial TI needs phased work and after-hours if the suite stays open.

6. Change orders. Extra soffits, niches, or fire caulking should be written and signed — not verbal add-ons at invoice time.

BH Drywall Metro Detroit provides written estimates, serves Wayne, Oakland, and Macomb counties, and answers questions at (313) 236-4558. Compare two bids using the same finish level and scope — then decide on crew quality and schedule, not just the lowest number.

Glossary

Drywall terms, explained in plain language

Whether you are comparing bids in Warren or planning a basement finish in Livonia, these are the terms BH Drywall Metro Detroit uses on every estimate — so you know exactly what you are paying for.

Hang — screwing gypsum board to studs or ceilings. "Hang only" means board is up but not taped or finished.

Level 4 / Level 5 finish — Level 4 is standard paint-ready in normal light. Level 5 adds a skim coat for critical lighting, glossy paint, or wallpaper.

Mud / compound — joint compound used to tape seams, cover screws, and skim walls.

Tape — paper or mesh embedded in mud over joints and inside corners.

Texture — orange peel, knockdown, skip trowel, or smooth. We match existing texture from a sample board before blending repairs.

Flood cut — horizontal cut in drywall after water damage, typically 12–24" above the wet line, before drying and replacement.

Metal stud framing — non-load-bearing partition walls common in commercial TI and basement builds.

Punch list — final walkthrough items — nail pops, corner bead, touch-up mud — before paint.
